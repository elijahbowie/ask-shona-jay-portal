# Medical / Health Reimbursement Strategy

> Educational guidance for Beyond Freedom Financial clients. Not personalized tax, legal, payroll, or state-law advice. Confirm your specific facts with your advisor before acting.

## Overview

The medical/health reimbursement strategy lets your business pay for medical expenses -- including health insurance premiums, out-of-pocket medical costs, and related healthcare spending -- in a way that is tax-deductible to the business and either tax-free or above-the-line deductible for you as the owner. Instead of paying healthcare costs purely from after-tax personal dollars, the right structure shifts those costs into the business, reducing both income tax and, in some cases, self-employment or payroll tax. The specific mechanism available to you depends on your entity type, how many employees you have, and whether you offer group health insurance. This playbook covers the four main paths for business owners: the S-corporation 2% shareholder health insurance deduction, the Qualified Small Employer Health Reimbursement Arrangement (QSEHRA), the Individual Coverage Health Reimbursement Arrangement (ICHRA), and the traditional employer-sponsored Health Reimbursement Arrangement (HRA) integrated with a group health plan.

## Who This Applies To

This strategy is relevant for:

- **S-corporation owner-employees who own more than 2% of the S-corp** and pay for health insurance personally or through the company. A specific W-2 inclusion and above-the-line deduction path is available that recovers income-tax benefit on the premiums.
- **Small business owners with fewer than 50 full-time equivalent employees who do not offer group health insurance** and want to reimburse employees (including themselves if structured correctly) for individual health insurance premiums and medical expenses. A QSEHRA is the primary tool.
- **Employers of any size who want to offer reimbursement for individual health insurance** instead of a traditional group plan. An ICHRA can be offered alongside or instead of a group plan, with no annual dollar cap on employer contributions.
- **Employers who already offer a group health plan** and want to add a traditional integrated HRA to help employees cover deductibles, copays, and other out-of-pocket costs.
- **C-corporation owner-employees and non-owner employees** whose employer can provide health benefits and HRA reimbursements fully deductible to the corporation and tax-free to the employee under the most favorable rules.
- **Sole proprietors and single-member LLCs taxed as sole proprietors** who pay for their own health insurance and may deduct premiums above-the-line on Schedule 1 of Form 1040 under IRC Section 162(l).

## When This Is Not A Fit

- **S-corp owners who want to use a QSEHRA:** S-corporation 2% shareholders are explicitly excluded from participating in a QSEHRA (per IRC Section 1372 and IRS Notice 2017-67). They must use the W-2 inclusion and above-the-line deduction path instead.
- **Employers who want to offer a QSEHRA but also offer a group health plan or a standard HRA:** You cannot offer a QSEHRA to any employee who is also eligible for a group health plan or employer HRA. The QSEHRA is specifically designed for employers who do not offer group coverage.
- **Employers with 50 or more full-time equivalent employees who want to offer a QSEHRA:** The QSEHRA is available only to "eligible employers," defined as employers that are not "applicable large employers" (generally fewer than 50 FTEs). Large employers must use an ICHRA or a traditional HRA integrated with a group plan.
- **Businesses where the owner is not an employee:** A sole proprietor who is not also an employee cannot be covered by an employer HRA or QSEHRA. Sole proprietors deduct health insurance premiums directly on Schedule 1 using IRC Section 162(l).
- **Situations where the employee (or their spouse) is eligible for another employer's subsidized health plan:** An S-corp shareholder-employee loses the above-the-line deduction for any month in which they or their spouse were eligible for coverage under a subsidized employer-sponsored health plan. Confirm eligibility month by month.
- **Business owners who cannot demonstrate the S-corp established and paid for the coverage:** For the 2% shareholder deduction to work, the S-corporation must establish the health plan (either by purchasing the policy in the company's name or by reimbursing the shareholder and including the premium in W-2 wages). If the corporation had no role, the deduction is not available.

## Key Terms

**Health Reimbursement Arrangement (HRA):** An employer-funded account that reimburses employees (and covered family members) for qualified medical expenses as defined in IRC Section 213(d). The employer contributes; employees cannot make their own contributions. Reimbursements are excluded from the employee's gross income under IRC Sections 106 and 105(b) and are deductible by the employer. HRAs are not available to self-employed individuals (sole proprietors) for their own coverage.

**QSEHRA (Qualified Small Employer Health Reimbursement Arrangement):** A type of HRA available only to eligible small employers (generally fewer than 50 full-time equivalent employees) who do not offer a group health plan or standard HRA to any employee. Employers may reimburse employees for individual health insurance premiums and out-of-pocket medical expenses up to an annual dollar cap. Governed by IRC Section 9831(d) and Notice 2017-67. S-corp 2% shareholders are ineligible.

**ICHRA (Individual Coverage HRA):** A type of HRA that allows employers of any size to reimburse employees for individual health insurance premiums and other qualified medical expenses, with no annual dollar cap on employer contributions. Available since January 1, 2020, under final regulations issued June 20, 2019 (84 FR 28888). Employees must be enrolled in individual health insurance or Medicare to receive reimbursements.

**Integrated HRA (traditional employer HRA):** An HRA offered by an employer alongside a group health plan that covers the same employees. Reimburses employees for out-of-pocket costs (deductibles, copays, coinsurance) that the group plan does not cover. No annual dollar cap on employer contributions. Employees do not need to enroll in individual coverage because they already have the group plan.

**Excepted Benefit HRA:** A limited HRA available to employees who are offered (but may not enroll in) a traditional group health plan. Maximum benefit for 2026 is $2,200 per plan year (per Rev. Proc. 2025-19). Can reimburse COBRA and short-term premiums and certain other excepted benefits. Not available to employees not offered the employer's primary group plan.

**IRC Section 106:** The Internal Revenue Code provision that excludes employer contributions to accident and health plans (including HRA contributions) from an employee's gross income.

**IRC Section 105(b):** The Code provision that excludes amounts received by an employee as reimbursements for medical care under an employer-provided accident or health plan from the employee's gross income, to the extent the expenses are actually for medical care.

**IRC Section 213(d):** Defines "medical care" -- the expenses an HRA, HSA, or FSA can reimburse tax-free. Includes payments for diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention of disease; payments for medical equipment; insurance premiums for medical, dental, and qualifying long-term care coverage; transportation to medical care; and certain other expenses. Does not include cosmetic procedures (unless correcting a deformity or disease), non-prescription drugs (other than insulin), toiletries, or general wellness expenses.

**IRC Section 162(l):** The Code provision allowing self-employed individuals (including sole proprietors and S-corp 2% shareholders) to deduct health insurance premiums as an above-the-line deduction in arriving at adjusted gross income (AGI), rather than as an itemized deduction. This deduction cannot exceed the net profit or income from the trade or business that established the plan.

**Above-the-line deduction:** A deduction subtracted from gross income to reach adjusted gross income (AGI). Above-the-line deductions are more valuable than itemized deductions because they reduce AGI regardless of whether you take the standard deduction, and they can reduce income-sensitive phase-outs.

**2% shareholder:** For S-corporation purposes, any person who owns (directly or constructively under IRC Section 318) more than 2% of the outstanding stock of the S-corp, or more than 2% of the total combined voting power. A 2% shareholder is treated as a partner (not as an employee) for purposes of most employee fringe benefits under IRC Section 1372.

**HDHP (High-Deductible Health Plan):** A health insurance plan with a minimum annual deductible and maximum annual out-of-pocket amount set by IRS each year. An individual must be covered by an HDHP (and not have certain other coverage) to be eligible to contribute to an HSA. For 2026: minimum deductible is $1,700 for self-only coverage or $3,400 for family coverage; maximum out-of-pocket is $8,500 for self-only or $17,000 for family coverage (per Rev. Proc. 2025-19).

**HSA (Health Savings Account):** A tax-advantaged account that eligible individuals (those enrolled in a qualifying HDHP) can use to save pre-tax money for qualified medical expenses. Contributions are deductible (or excluded from income if made by an employer), earnings grow tax-free, and withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax-free. For 2026: maximum contribution is $4,400 for self-only coverage or $8,750 for family coverage (per Rev. Proc. 2025-19). Additional $1,000 catch-up contribution allowed for individuals age 55 or older. Unlike an FSA or HRA, HSA funds belong to the individual and roll over indefinitely.

**Health FSA (Health Flexible Spending Arrangement):** An employer benefit plan under which an employee sets aside pre-tax salary (salary reduction) to pay for qualified medical expenses. For plan years beginning in 2026, the employee contribution limit is $3,400, with a maximum carryover of $680 to the next plan year (per Rev. Proc. 2025-32). Unlike an HSA, a health FSA is employer-sponsored and use-or-lose (subject to the carryover or grace period rule).

**Form W-2, Box 1:** Reports total taxable wages paid to an employee. For S-corp 2% shareholders, health insurance premiums paid by the S-corp must be included in Box 1 as taxable wages for federal income tax purposes.

**Form W-2, Box 12, Code DD:** Used by employers to report the cost of employer-sponsored health coverage (informational only; not taxable to the employee). Separate from Code FF used for QSEHRA.

**Form W-2, Box 12, Code FF:** Used by employers to report the amount reimbursed under a QSEHRA arrangement for the calendar year. The amount in Box 12 Code FF is used by employees to calculate any reduction in the Premium Tax Credit if they also received marketplace coverage.

**Form 7206:** The IRS form (introduced for 2023 and later tax years) that S-corp 2% shareholders and other self-employed individuals use to calculate the self-employed health insurance deduction under IRC Section 162(l). Reports on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 17.

**Schedule 1 (Form 1040), Line 17:** Where the self-employed health insurance deduction (calculated on Form 7206) is reported as an above-the-line deduction.

**Applicable Large Employer (ALE):** An employer that employed an average of at least 50 full-time equivalent employees in the prior calendar year. ALEs have Affordable Care Act employer mandate obligations under IRC Section 4980H and cannot use a QSEHRA.

**IRC Section 105(h) nondiscrimination rules:** Rules that prohibit self-insured medical reimbursement plans (including traditional HRAs) from discriminating in favor of highly compensated employees with respect to eligibility or benefits. If a plan is discriminatory, highly compensated employees must include the excess reimbursements in gross income.

**Premium Tax Credit (PTC):** A refundable tax credit that helps eligible individuals and families pay premiums for health insurance purchased through a marketplace (exchange). Employees who are offered an affordable ICHRA cannot claim the PTC for that coverage period (they must opt out of the ICHRA to potentially claim the PTC).

**Direct Primary Care (DPC) arrangement:** A healthcare delivery model in which a patient pays a periodic flat fee directly to a primary care physician for a defined set of primary care services, without insurance involvement. Effective January 1, 2026 (OBBBA Section 71307), individuals enrolled in a qualifying DPC arrangement may also contribute to an HSA, and HSA funds may be used to pay DPC fees.

**IRC Section 9831(d):** The Internal Revenue Code provision that created the QSEHRA, allowing small employers to offer this type of HRA without violating ACA group health plan requirements.

## Required Client Inputs

Gather these facts before choosing a path and implementing the strategy:

1. **Entity type and tax classification:** Is the business an S-corp, C-corp, partnership, sole proprietorship, or single-member LLC? Which path is available depends on entity type.
2. **Ownership percentage (S-corp):** Do you own more than 2% of the S-corp's stock or voting power, directly or constructively? This determines whether you are a "2% shareholder" subject to special rules.
3. **Number of full-time equivalent employees:** Count your full-time and part-time employees to determine if you are an applicable large employer (50+ FTEs). This gates QSEHRA eligibility.
4. **Whether you currently offer a group health plan or standard HRA to any employees:** QSEHRA eligibility requires that no employee be offered a group plan or standard employer HRA.
5. **Current health insurance situation:** Who pays the premiums (you personally or the company)? Whose name is the policy in? What is the monthly premium amount?
6. **W-2 payroll setup:** Is the S-corp currently running payroll and issuing W-2s to the owner-employee? The 2% shareholder deduction requires W-2 inclusion of the premiums.
7. **Employees covered or to be covered:** Which employees do you want to cover under a QSEHRA, ICHRA, or HRA? Do any have family members to cover?
8. **State of organization and operating states:** Some states impose additional requirements on HRA arrangements and health benefit plans.
9. **Availability of other subsidized coverage:** Does you or your spouse have access to health coverage through another employer's subsidized plan? This affects the 2% shareholder deduction month by month.
10. **Annual health insurance and medical spending amounts:** How much do you and covered employees spend on premiums and out-of-pocket medical costs per year? This helps size the contribution.

## Required Documents And Records

### For the S-corp 2% shareholder health insurance deduction

- **Proof the S-corp established the coverage:** Either the insurance policy in the company's name, or documentation showing the company reimbursed the shareholder for premiums paid personally and included the amount in W-2 wages.
- **W-2 showing the premium amount in Box 1:** The S-corp's payroll records and the issued W-2 must show the premium amount included as taxable wages.
- **Form 7206:** Completed annually to calculate and claim the above-the-line deduction on Schedule 1.
- **Monthly eligibility records:** Documentation that you were not eligible for subsidized coverage through another employer for each month you claim the deduction.
- **Invoices or statements from the insurance carrier:** Showing the monthly premium amounts paid.

### For QSEHRA

- **Written QSEHRA plan document:** A formal written arrangement that meets the requirements of IRC Section 9831(d). Must be adopted before the first reimbursement is made. Identifies the employer, eligible employees, maximum annual benefit (up to $6,450 for self-only or $13,100 for family in 2026), and the start date.
- **Notice to eligible employees:** The employer must provide written notice to eligible employees at least 90 days before the start of each plan year (or, for new employees, no later than the date of first eligibility) explaining the maximum annual amount, that employees should share QSEHRA information when applying for PTC, and that the employee is responsible for maintaining minimum essential coverage.
- **Proof of minimum essential coverage:** Employees must provide proof of individual health insurance coverage before receiving reimbursements. Keep copies of proof of coverage (such as insurance cards or marketplace enrollment confirmation) in your plan records.
- **Reimbursement requests and supporting documentation:** Receipts, invoices, or Explanation of Benefits (EOB) statements documenting each medical expense or premium payment being reimbursed. Keep all substantiation records.
- **Form W-2, Box 12, Code FF reporting:** The QSEHRA amount reimbursed for the calendar year must be reported in Box 12 of the employee's W-2 using Code FF.

### For ICHRA

- **Written ICHRA plan document:** A formal written plan document adopted before the plan effective date. States the employer, eligible employee classes, annual contribution amount, start date, and the requirement that employees must be enrolled in individual health insurance or Medicare.
- **Notice to employees:** Written notice provided to eligible employees at least 90 days before the plan year starts (or upon hire for new employees) explaining how the ICHRA works, the annual amount available, and how receiving the ICHRA affects eligibility for the Premium Tax Credit.
- **Proof of individual health insurance enrollment:** Each eligible employee must provide documentation of enrollment in individual health coverage or Medicare before receiving reimbursements.
- **Reimbursement documentation:** Same as QSEHRA -- receipts, invoices, or EOBs for each expense reimbursed.

### For traditional employer HRA integrated with group health plan

- **Written HRA plan document:** Formal plan document establishing the HRA, the covered employee population, the annual contribution amount, eligible expenses, and the group health plan it integrates with.
- **Group health plan enrollment confirmation:** The HRA must be offered only to employees enrolled in the employer's group health plan (or a spouse/dependent covered under the group plan).
- **Reimbursement documentation:** Receipts and EOBs for each expense reimbursed.

### Retention

Keep all plan documents, reimbursement records, supporting receipts, and employee notices for a minimum of 6 years from the plan year end, or at least 3 years from the date of the related tax return filing, whichever is longer. IRS audit risk for employment-related arrangements warrants the longer period. Keep employee proof-of-coverage documents for the duration of their employment plus 6 years.

## Step-By-Step Implementation

Work through the applicable path below. Start with Step 1 to determine which path applies.

---

### Step 1 -- Determine which path applies to your situation

Review the table below and confirm your path with your advisor:

| Your Situation | Applicable Path |
|---|---|
| S-corp owner with more than 2% stock ownership | Path A: S-corp 2% shareholder health insurance deduction |
| Fewer than 50 FTE employees, no group health plan, S-corp 2% shareholders excluded | Path B: QSEHRA |
| Any size employer, want to reimburse individual health premiums without a group plan | Path C: ICHRA |
| Employer with a group health plan, want to help employees cover out-of-pocket costs | Path D: Traditional integrated HRA |

It is possible to operate more than one path simultaneously (for example, an S-corp with the owner on Path A and W-2 employees on Path C), but confirm the combination is permissible with your advisor before adopting both.

---

### Path A: S-Corp 2% Shareholder Health Insurance Deduction

**Step A1 -- Establish the health plan through the S-corporation.**
The S-corp must either: (a) purchase the health insurance policy in the company's name and cover the shareholder, or (b) the shareholder purchases a policy individually and the S-corp reimburses the premiums and includes the full reimbursed amount in the shareholder's W-2 wages. Per IRS Notice 2008-1, either method works as long as the S-corp ultimately pays or reimburses the premium and the amount is included in W-2 compensation.

**Step A2 -- Include the premium amount in W-2 wages.**
The premium amount must be added to the shareholder's W-2, Box 1 (federal taxable wages). It is NOT subject to Social Security or Medicare taxes (Boxes 3 and 5 remain unchanged), and it is NOT subject to federal unemployment tax (FUTA) -- provided the premiums are paid under a plan or system that makes provision for all or a class of employees (per the IRS S-Corporation Compensation and Medical Insurance Issues guidance). Work with your payroll provider to code this correctly. The IRS recommends reporting the amount in Box 14 of the W-2 as "S-corp health ins." for clarity, though Box 14 is informational.

**Step A3 -- Confirm monthly eligibility for the deduction.**
The deduction under IRC Section 162(l) is disallowed for any month in which you (the shareholder-employee) or your spouse were eligible to participate in any subsidized health care plan through another employer. Check eligibility month by month. If you were eligible for another subsidized plan for any month, that month's premium is not deductible under Section 162(l) for that month (it may still qualify as a Schedule A itemized medical expense subject to the 7.5% AGI floor).

**Step A4 -- Calculate the deduction on Form 7206.**
At tax time, complete Form 7206 (Self-Employed Health Insurance Deduction). Enter the total premiums included in your W-2 Box 1 for the year, adjusted for any months of ineligibility. The deduction cannot exceed your net income from the S-corp (your W-2 wages plus your share of S-corp income, reduced by certain items). The Form 7206 instructions walk through this calculation.

**Step A5 -- Report the deduction on Schedule 1, Line 17.**
The amount calculated on Form 7206 flows to Schedule 1 (Form 1040), Line 17, as an above-the-line deduction. This reduces your AGI and your federal income tax. It does not reduce your self-employment tax (S-corp shareholders do not pay self-employment tax on S-corp income) and does not reduce the Social Security or Medicare taxes already paid on the W-2 wages.

**Step A6 -- Maintain records.**
Keep the W-2, insurance invoices or statements, and a record of the months in which you were not eligible for any other subsidized plan. Retain for at least 3 years from the return filing date.

---

### Path B: QSEHRA (Qualified Small Employer Health Reimbursement Arrangement)

**Step B1 -- Confirm eligibility.**
Verify that: (a) your business had fewer than 50 full-time equivalent employees in the prior year; (b) you do not offer any group health plan or traditional HRA to any employees; and (c) no S-corp 2% shareholders will be included in the QSEHRA. Confirm with your advisor.

**Step B2 -- Determine the annual benefit amount.**
Decide how much the company will make available per year per employee for self-only coverage and for employees with family coverage. The maximum for 2026 is $6,450 for self-only ($537.50 per month) and $13,100 for family coverage ($1,091.67 per month) (per Rev. Proc. 2025-32). You may offer less than the maximum.

**Step B3 -- Draft and adopt the written QSEHRA plan document.**
The plan document must be adopted before the first reimbursement. It must state: the employer name, the effective date, which employees are eligible (all eligible employees must generally be offered the QSEHRA on the same terms; limited exceptions exist for employees who have worked less than 90 days, are under age 25, are part-time or seasonal, are covered by a collective bargaining agreement, or are nonresident aliens with no U.S. income), the maximum annual reimbursement amount, the types of eligible expenses (health insurance premiums and IRC Section 213(d) medical expenses), and the requirement that employees provide proof of minimum essential coverage before reimbursement. Your advisor should review the plan document before adoption.

**Step B4 -- Send the required employee notice.**
At least 90 days before the start of the plan year (or by the employee's date of first eligibility for new hires), provide each eligible employee a written notice explaining: the annual maximum reimbursement amount; that the employee must have minimum essential coverage to receive reimbursements; that the employee should report the QSEHRA amount to the health insurance marketplace when applying for a Premium Tax Credit (the PTC will be reduced by the QSEHRA amount); and what happens if the employee does not have minimum essential coverage.

**Step B5 -- Collect proof of employee health insurance coverage.**
Before issuing any reimbursement, obtain documentation from the employee confirming enrollment in a qualifying individual health plan or other minimum essential coverage (such as Medicare, Medicaid, or a marketplace plan). Keep copies.

**Step B6 -- Process reimbursement requests.**
When an employee submits a reimbursement request with a receipt, invoice, or EOB, verify the expense is an eligible IRC Section 213(d) medical expense or a qualifying health insurance premium, verify the employee has current minimum essential coverage, and issue the reimbursement. Keep all documentation.

**Step B7 -- Report QSEHRA amounts on Form W-2, Box 12, Code FF.**
At year-end, report the total QSEHRA amount available to (not just paid to) each eligible employee in Box 12 of their W-2 using Code FF. This is required even if the employee did not use the full amount. This amount is not included in Box 1 wages.

---

### Path C: ICHRA (Individual Coverage HRA)

**Step C1 -- Confirm eligibility and design the plan.**
An ICHRA can be offered by employers of any size. Confirm with your advisor that no conflicting group health plan coverage would disqualify employees from participating (employees receiving ICHRA reimbursements cannot use the ICHRA period as the basis for a marketplace Premium Tax Credit unless the ICHRA is "unaffordable" and they opt out). Decide which class of employees will receive the ICHRA (ICHRA rules allow different classes of employees to receive different benefits or no ICHRA).

**Step C2 -- Determine the annual contribution amount.**
There is no dollar cap on ICHRA contributions. You decide the annual amount per employee (or per employee class). The amount must be the same for all employees within a given class (with permitted variations based on age and family size).

**Step C3 -- Draft and adopt the written ICHRA plan document.**
Same general approach as QSEHRA. Must be adopted before the effective date. Must state eligible employees, annual benefit amount, requirement that employees maintain individual health insurance or Medicare, and eligible expenses (IRC Section 213(d) medical expenses and individual insurance premiums). Your advisor should review before adoption.

**Step C4 -- Send the required employee notice.**
Provide written notice to each eligible employee at least 90 days before the plan year starts (or at hire for new employees) explaining how the ICHRA works, the annual amount, and how participation affects PTC eligibility.

**Step C5 -- Collect proof of individual health insurance enrollment.**
Before reimbursing, verify and document that the employee is enrolled in qualifying individual health insurance or Medicare.

**Step C6 -- Process reimbursement requests and maintain records.**
Issue reimbursements upon receipt of documented claims. Keep all substantiation. There is no W-2 Box 12 Code FF requirement for ICHRA (unlike QSEHRA), but the reimbursements must not be included in employee wages.

---

### Path D: Traditional Employer HRA (Integrated with Group Health Plan)

**Step D1 -- Confirm you offer a group health plan.**
This path is only available if you offer a group health plan to the same employees who will receive the HRA.

**Step D2 -- Design the HRA: annual amount and eligible expenses.**
Determine the annual HRA contribution per employee (or per employee class). No dollar cap. Decide whether the HRA reimburses only certain expenses (such as deductibles and copays) or all IRC Section 213(d) expenses.

**Step D3 -- Adopt a written HRA plan document.**
Draft and adopt the written plan before the effective date. Must confirm integration with the group health plan and specify eligible expenses and the annual amount. Have your advisor review it.

**Step D4 -- Comply with IRC Section 105(h) nondiscrimination rules.**
The HRA must not discriminate in favor of highly compensated employees. Work with your advisor to confirm the eligibility and benefits pass the nondiscrimination tests.

**Step D5 -- Process claims and maintain records.**
Issue reimbursements upon receipt of qualified expense documentation. Keep all records.

---

### All Paths: Annual Maintenance

**Step Final-1 -- Review and update limits each January.**
HRA contribution limits for QSEHRA are indexed for inflation annually. Check for updated QSEHRA limits each fall (the IRS publishes them in a revenue procedure, typically in October). Update ICHRA and HRA contribution amounts if desired.

**Step Final-2 -- Confirm W-2 reporting is correct.**
For Path A: confirm the W-2 premium amount is in Box 1 (not Boxes 3 and 5). For Path B: confirm Box 12 Code FF is completed. For Paths C and D: confirm reimbursements are excluded from Box 1 wages.

**Step Final-3 -- File all required forms.**
Complete Form 7206 for Path A. Confirm no separate Form 5500 annual report is required for the HRA arrangement (small employer HRAs with fewer than 100 participants generally qualify for the small plan exemption; confirm with your advisor and ERISA counsel).

| ICHRA: adopt written plan document | Before the plan effective date |

**Recurring rhythm:**

- **Monthly:** S-corp owners running payroll should confirm the health insurance premium is included in each payroll period's W-2 Box 1 reporting, or adjust at year-end with the payroll provider. QSEHRA/ICHRA plan administrators should process employee reimbursement requests as they arrive and maintain current proof of coverage on file.
- **Annually (October):** Check for updated QSEHRA contribution limits for the next year. Update the written plan and employee notice amounts if the limit increased and you want to match it.
- **Annually (January):** Confirm W-2 Box 12 Code FF reporting for QSEHRA. Confirm Form 7206 preparation for S-corp shareholders. Update written plan documents if limits changed.

S-corp 2% shareholders are treated as self-employed under IRC Section 1372. The interaction between ICHRA and the 2% shareholder rules is not definitively addressed in a single published IRS guidance document as of the date of this playbook. [Confirm with your advisor whether an S-corp 2% shareholder can participate in an ICHRA and whether it satisfies the "established by the employer" requirement for the Section 162(l) deduction.]

**Note on self-employed health insurance deduction limitation:** The IRC Section 162(l) deduction cannot exceed the taxpayer's net profit from the business that established the plan. If the S-corp generates a net loss for the year, or if the shareholder's W-2 wages from the S-corp are less than the premiums, the deduction may be limited. Form 7206 performs this calculation.

## Entity, Payroll, State, And Current-Year Caveats

### Entity-specific caveats

- **S-corp 2% shareholders are excluded from QSEHRA:** Do not include any person who owns more than 2% of the S-corp stock in a QSEHRA. If you try to include them, reimbursements received by the 2% shareholder must be included in their W-2 gross income and become taxable. Rely on Path A instead.
- **S-corp must be paying the shareholder reasonable compensation on payroll:** The health insurance premium inclusion only works if the S-corp is already running payroll with W-2 wages for the shareholder. If you have not established payroll, fix that first.
- **C-corporations:** A C-corp can provide health insurance and HRA benefits to employee-shareholders with the most favorable treatment because C-corp owner-employees are not subject to the 2% shareholder rules. The corporation deducts the cost; the employee-shareholder is not taxed on the benefit (if the plan does not discriminate).
- **Sole proprietors and single-member LLCs (disregarded entities):** Cannot use a QSEHRA, ICHRA, or traditional HRA for themselves. Deduct health insurance premiums directly on Schedule 1 under IRC Section 162(l). Sole proprietors CAN establish a QSEHRA or ICHRA for their W-2 employees.
- **Partnerships and multi-member LLCs taxed as partnerships:** Partners cannot receive employer-provided health benefits tax-free. Under the partner-not-employee rule and Rev. Rul. 91-26, health insurance premiums paid by a partnership on behalf of a partner are treated as guaranteed payments under IRC Section 707(c) and are includible in the partner's gross income under IRC Section 61(a); the partner cannot exclude the premiums under IRC Section 106. Partners who are more than 2% owners must include health insurance premiums in gross income. [Confirm the specific tax treatment for your partnership structure with your advisor.] A partnership can establish a QSEHRA for its W-2 employees who are not partners.

### Payroll caveats

- **QSEHRA reimbursements are NOT wages:** Do not run QSEHRA or ICHRA reimbursements through payroll as wages. They are not subject to federal income tax withholding, Social Security, Medicare, or FUTA taxes when the plan is properly structured. Running them as wages loses the tax exclusion.
- **S-corp health premiums: FICA/FUTA exclusion is conditional:** The exclusion of S-corp 2% shareholder health premiums from Social Security, Medicare, and FUTA taxes applies when the premiums are paid under a plan or system that makes provision for all or a class of employees. This condition is typically met in a standard one-owner S-corp setup but must be confirmed with your payroll provider.
- **S-corp health premiums require correct W-2 Box coding:** If your payroll provider incorrectly includes the health premium in Boxes 3 and 5 (Social Security and Medicare wages), you have overpaid FICA taxes. Review the W-2 coding with your payroll provider before the end of each year.

### State caveats

- **State income tax conformity:** Most states conform to the federal treatment of HRA reimbursements and the Section 162(l) self-employed health insurance deduction, but not all states have conformed to all OBBBA and ACA changes. Confirm your state's treatment with your advisor, especially if you are in a state that has its own individual health insurance market rules (California, New York, Massachusetts, and others have specific rules affecting QSEHRA and ICHRA interactions with state-run exchanges).
- **State employer mandate:** Some states (including California, Massachusetts, New Jersey, and others) impose their own employer health insurance requirements. A QSEHRA or ICHRA may or may not satisfy the state's requirement. Confirm with your advisor.
- **State-specific HRA rules:** Some states require separate registration or notification for employer health benefit arrangements. Confirm local requirements.

### Current-year caveats (2026)

- **OBBBA HSA eligibility expansions (effective January 1, 2026):** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (Public Law 119-21, signed July 4, 2025) permanently expanded HSA eligibility in two ways effective January 1, 2026: (1) bronze and catastrophic health plans available through an exchange are now HSA-compatible, regardless of whether they meet the standard HDHP definition; and (2) individuals enrolled in a qualifying direct primary care (DPC) arrangement may also contribute to an HSA and may use HSA funds to pay DPC fees. These changes create new planning opportunities for business owners who purchase bronze-level plans for themselves or their employees. Per IRS Notice 2026-05.
- **OBBBA telehealth HSA rule made permanent:** The ability to use telehealth and remote care services before meeting the HDHP deductible without losing HSA eligibility was extended permanently under the OBBBA, effective for plan years beginning on or after January 1, 2025. Per IRS Notice 2026-05.
- **TCJA and OBBBA: miscellaneous itemized deductions remain suspended:** The OBBBA permanently eliminated the miscellaneous itemized deduction for unreimbursed employee business expenses. This does not directly affect medical expense deductions (which are allowed as itemized deductions under IRC Section 213 subject to the 7.5% AGI floor), but it reinforces that employer-paid medical reimbursements are far more valuable than out-of-pocket spending.
- **Premium Tax Credit rules changed:** OBBBA Sections 71301-71305 eliminated limitations on repayment obligations for excess advance Premium Tax Credit payments for tax years beginning after December 31, 2025. This affects employees who receive marketplace coverage while also receiving a QSEHRA -- they need to properly disclose the QSEHRA amount to the marketplace to avoid repayment issues.
- **The Section 162(l) self-employed health insurance deduction has no scheduled sunset:** It is a permanent provision of the Internal Revenue Code. No current-year change is expected.

## Common Mistakes

1. **S-corp: not including health premiums in W-2 Box 1.** If the S-corp pays the premium but does not add it to the shareholder's W-2 taxable wages, the premium is not deductible by the shareholder under Section 162(l). Without the W-2 inclusion, the deduction is lost for that year.

2. **S-corp: including health premiums in Boxes 3 and 5 (FICA wages).** Health insurance premiums for S-corp 2% shareholders are NOT subject to Social Security or Medicare taxes -- provided the premiums are paid under a plan or system that makes provision for all or a class of employees (per the IRS S-Corporation Compensation and Medical Insurance Issues guidance). If your payroll provider codes them as FICA wages, you are overpaying payroll taxes. Correct this before W-2s are finalized each January.

3. **S-corp: claiming the deduction for months when you or your spouse were eligible for another subsidized plan.** The Section 162(l) deduction is disallowed month by month if you had access to subsidized employer coverage elsewhere. Claiming it for those months is incorrect and could be flagged on audit.

4. **QSEHRA: including S-corp 2% shareholders in the plan.** Any reimbursement made to a 2% shareholder under a QSEHRA must be included in their W-2 as taxable wages, eliminating the tax benefit. Keep 2% shareholders on Path A.

5. **QSEHRA: failing to send the required employee notice.** Not providing the 90-day advance written notice (or timely notice for new hires) violates the QSEHRA statute. Under IRC Section 6652(o), the penalty for failing to provide the required written notice is $50 per employee per incident of failure, capped at $2,500 per calendar year -- unless the failure is due to reasonable cause and not willful neglect (per IRS Notice 2017-20 and IRM 20.1.10).

6. **QSEHRA: not verifying employee proof of coverage before reimbursing.** Reimbursing an employee who does not have minimum essential coverage violates QSEHRA rules and may subject the reimbursement to inclusion in the employee's income.

7. **Not adopting a written plan document before the first reimbursement.** For any HRA path, the plan must be adopted in writing before any money is reimbursed. Backdating the plan document is improper and can void the arrangement retroactively.

8. **Confusing an HSA with an HRA.** An HSA belongs to the employee, is funded by employee and/or employer contributions, requires HDHP coverage, and has no "use it or lose it" rule. An HRA belongs to the employer, is funded solely by the employer, does not require an HDHP (for most HRA types), and unused amounts may or may not carry over depending on the plan design. These are separate tools with different rules.

9. **Trying to offer a QSEHRA alongside a group health plan.** If any employee is offered or covered by the employer's group health plan, the employer cannot offer a QSEHRA to any employee. The QSEHRA is only for employers with no group plan.

10. **Reimbursing non-qualifying expenses.** HRAs can only reimburse IRC Section 213(d) medical expenses (and qualifying health insurance premiums, for QSEHRA and ICHRA). Reimbursing gym memberships, vitamins, or other general wellness items that are not 213(d) expenses turns the reimbursement into taxable income. Review the eligible expense list and maintain it in your plan document.

11. **Not updating QSEHRA plan documents when limits change.** QSEHRA limits are indexed annually. If you want to offer the full statutory maximum, update the written plan and employee notice each year when new limits are announced.

## Advisor Review Gates

Do not proceed through the following decisions without first confirming with your advisor:

1. **Before choosing a path:** Confirm which path (A, B, C, or D) applies to your specific entity type, ownership percentage, employee count, and current health insurance setup. The paths have technical eligibility requirements that are easy to misapply without professional review.

2. **Before adopting any written plan document:** Have your advisor (and, for ERISA purposes, an attorney if you have employees other than yourself) review the plan document and notice before you adopt it and before any reimbursements are made.

3. **S-corp shareholders: before finalizing year-end payroll:** Confirm with your advisor and payroll provider that health insurance premiums are in W-2 Box 1 and excluded from Boxes 3 and 5. Errors must be corrected before W-2s are issued.

4. **Before adding health benefits to an existing arrangement:** If you already have any HRA, group plan, or cafeteria plan in place, confirm with your advisor that the new arrangement does not violate the "no group health plan" rule for QSEHRA or the integration requirements for ICHRA.

5. **If any employee (or you) had access to another employer's subsidized health plan:** Confirm the month-by-month eligibility impact on the Section 162(l) deduction or on QSEHRA/ICHRA reimbursement eligibility.

6. **State compliance:** Before the first reimbursement, confirm your state's treatment of the chosen HRA type and whether any state employer health mandate applies.

7. **ICHRA affordability calculations (if you have 50+ FTEs):** If you are an applicable large employer and offer an ICHRA, you must confirm the ICHRA is "affordable" under ACA employer mandate rules using the lowest cost silver plan benchmark. This is a technical calculation; confirm with your advisor before the plan year starts.

8. **HSA interaction:** If any employee will contribute to an HSA, confirm the health plan they are enrolled in is HDHP-qualifying (or qualifies under the 2026 OBBBA expansions for bronze/catastrophic plans or DPC arrangements). Receiving HRA reimbursements for non-premium expenses from most HRAs disqualifies an individual from contributing to an HSA for that period. Your advisor must evaluate whether a "limited-purpose HRA" (covering only permitted expenses such as dental and vision) is appropriate if HSA eligibility is a priority.

## Before You Act

**Stop before you reimburse, adopt a plan, adjust your payroll, or file a return.**

Medical and health reimbursement strategies are among the highest-value planning tools available to business owners, but they are also among the most technically precise. The wrong path, a missing written plan document, an incorrect W-2 coding, or a reimbursement made to an ineligible participant can convert a tax-free benefit into taxable income and trigger excise taxes, amended returns, and penalties.

Before you act:

- Confirm your specific entity structure, ownership percentage, employee count, and current health insurance situation with your advisor. The available path depends on facts specific to your business.
- Have your advisor review any plan document before you adopt it and before any money moves.
- Confirm with your payroll provider that W-2 Box coding is correct (for S-corp shareholders: Box 1 inclusion, no Boxes 3 and 5; for QSEHRA: Box 12 Code FF; for HRA reimbursements: excluded from wages) before W-2s are finalized.
- Confirm your state's treatment with your advisor before the first reimbursement.
- Review the Medical Reimbursement Quick Reference Guide to understand the limits, eligible expenses, and notice requirements before your advisor meeting.

This page and the attached Quick Reference Guide teach the strategy and the steps. Your advisor applies them to your specific facts, confirms eligibility, and reviews documentation before anything is filed or reimbursed.
