# Accountable Plan Reimbursements

> Educational guidance for Beyond Freedom Financial clients. Not personalized tax, legal, payroll, or state-law advice. Confirm your specific facts with your advisor before acting.

## Overview

An accountable plan is an IRS-approved arrangement through which your business reimburses you (as an employee or owner-employee) for legitimate business expenses you paid out of pocket, so those reimbursements are tax-free to you and fully deductible by the business. Instead of absorbing personal out-of-pocket costs -- or claiming those costs as unreimbursed deductions that no longer exist for most employees -- you route the expenses through the company, document them properly, and receive a reimbursement check that does not appear on your W-2 and is not subject to payroll tax. The result is a dollar-for-dollar shift of after-tax spending into pre-tax business deductions.

## Who This Applies To

This strategy is most valuable for:

- **S-corporation owner-employees:** You receive a W-2 salary from your S-corp. An accountable plan lets your S-corp reimburse you for home office, mileage, cell phone, internet, and other business costs tax-free and without payroll tax on either side.
- **C-corporation employees and owner-employees:** The same rules apply. The C-corp gets a deduction and the employee pays no income or payroll tax on the reimbursement.
- **Partnership and multi-member LLC employees:** Partnerships may establish accountable plans for employees (not partners); partners with home-office expenses may be covered if the partnership agreement requires it.
- **Sole proprietors with employees:** A sole proprietor can establish an accountable plan for employees (other than themselves, since a sole proprietor cannot be their own employee under IRS rules).
- **Any W-2 employee** working for a business that has not yet adopted an accountable plan and who incurs real business expenses the employer would be willing to reimburse.

## When This Is Not A Fit

- **Sole proprietors and single-member LLCs taxed as sole proprietors** cannot use an accountable plan for themselves. You deduct business expenses directly on Schedule C. An accountable plan only works where there is an employer-employee relationship.
- **Partners in a partnership (for themselves):** Partners are not employees for fringe-benefit purposes, so the accountable plan path is limited. A Guaranteed Payment approach or a specific provision in the partnership agreement is needed; consult your advisor.
- **Expenses with no genuine business purpose:** Reimbursing personal costs and calling them business expenses is fraud, not tax planning.
- **Businesses that are not willing to adopt the written plan and keep records:** Sloppy implementation converts the reimbursements into taxable wages plus penalties. If your entity will not commit to the documentation cadence, do not start.
- **2% S-corp shareholders for certain fringe benefits (not business expense reimbursements):** While 2% shareholders (anyone who directly or indirectly owns more than 2% of the S-corp's stock) are treated as partners, not employees, for purposes of many fringe benefits (such as employer-paid health insurance), they ARE able to receive tax-free business expense reimbursements under an accountable plan. The critical distinction: health/welfare benefits follow different rules, but ordinary business expense reimbursements (mileage, home office, phone, travel) that meet the three accountable plan requirements are excluded from the 2% shareholder's gross income as working condition fringe benefits. Confirm the specific fringe benefit treatment with your advisor.

## Key Terms

**Accountable plan:** An employer reimbursement arrangement that meets three IRS requirements (business connection, substantiation, and return of excess) so that reimbursements are excluded from the employee's gross income and not subject to payroll tax. Governed by Treasury Regulation 1.62-2 and Internal Revenue Code (IRC) Section 62(a)(2)(A).

**Business connection:** The reimbursed expense must be a deductible business expense under IRC Section 162 (ordinary and necessary business expenses) that the employee incurred while performing services as an employee. Personal expenses do not qualify.

**Substantiation:** Providing your employer with documentary evidence of each expense: the amount, date, place or description, and business purpose. This is "adequate accounting" in IRS language. For travel, meals, vehicle, and gift expenses governed by IRC Section 274(d), the substantiation requirements are especially strict.

**Return of excess:** Any advance or reimbursement that exceeds the amount actually spent on substantiated business expenses must be returned to the employer within a "reasonable period of time."

**Nonaccountable plan:** An arrangement that fails any one of the three accountable plan requirements. All payments under a nonaccountable plan are treated as taxable wages, reported on Form W-2, and subject to federal income tax withholding and payroll taxes (FICA).

**FICA:** Federal Insurance Contributions Act taxes -- the combination of Social Security tax and Medicare tax -- paid by both employer and employee on wages.

**W-2:** IRS Form W-2, Wage and Tax Statement, the annual form an employer gives each employee showing wages paid and taxes withheld. Proper accountable plan reimbursements are NOT reported as wages in Box 1 of the W-2.

**Form W-2 Box 12 Code L:** The IRS code used to report the nontaxable (substantiated) portion of mileage or per diem allowances paid under an accountable plan, when those allowances exceed the IRS-approved federal rate. The excess over the federal rate IS included in Box 1 as taxable wages.

**Per diem:** A fixed daily rate an employer pays instead of reimbursing actual lodging and meal costs. The IRS and GSA (General Services Administration) publish approved per diem rates; using amounts at or below those rates simplifies substantiation.

**Standard mileage rate:** An IRS-published rate per business mile driven that substitutes for calculating actual vehicle costs (fuel, depreciation, insurance, maintenance). Using this rate is optional; you can use actual costs instead.

**M&IE:** Meals and incidental expenses. "Incidental expenses" means fees and tips for porters, baggage carriers, and hotel staff. M&IE is the portion of the per diem rate covering food and incidentals (separate from lodging).

**Working condition fringe benefit:** Under IRC Section 132(d), property or services an employer provides to an employee that would be deductible by the employee as a business expense if the employee had paid for them. Properly substantiated accountable plan reimbursements qualify as working condition fringe benefits and are excluded from gross income.

**IRC Section 274(d):** A Code provision that imposes strict substantiation requirements for travel, meals, vehicle use, and gifts. Expenses in these categories require records showing amount, time, place, and business purpose. No deduction (or exclusion) is allowed without adequate records.

**Corporate resolution:** A formal written record of a decision made by a corporation's board of directors. S-corp owners adopting an accountable plan should pass a corporate resolution that authorizes and establishes the plan.

**Reasonable period of time (IRS safe harbor):** The IRS provides three bright-line timeframes that automatically qualify as "reasonable": (1) receive an advance within 30 days of incurring the expense; (2) submit substantiation within 60 days after paying or incurring the expense; (3) return any excess reimbursement within 120 days after paying or incurring the expense. Alternatively, employers may issue periodic statements at least quarterly asking employees to account for outstanding advances, with a 120-day window to comply after the statement.

**Schedule C:** The IRS tax form sole proprietors use to report business income and deductions. Sole proprietors deduct expenses on Schedule C rather than using an accountable plan.

**TCJA:** The Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017, which suspended the miscellaneous itemized deduction for unreimbursed employee business expenses from 2018 through 2025.

**OBBBA:** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed into law July 4, 2025 (Public Law 119-21), which permanently eliminated the miscellaneous itemized deduction for unreimbursed employee business expenses for most employees. This makes employer accountable plans the primary remaining path for W-2 employees to obtain tax benefit from business expenses.

## Required Client Inputs

Gather these facts before setting up an accountable plan:

1. **Entity type and tax classification:** Is the business an S-corp, C-corp, partnership, or sole proprietorship? Confirm with your advisor because the mechanics differ.
2. **Your role:** Are you a W-2 employee or owner-employee of the entity?
3. **Ownership percentage:** If S-corp, what is your stock ownership percentage? (Relevant for fringe benefit rules affecting 2% shareholders.)
4. **Existing payroll setup:** Does the company currently run payroll and issue W-2s? An accountable plan requires a payroll structure.
5. **Categories of expenses you incur for the business:** Home office, vehicle/mileage, cell phone, internet, professional subscriptions, travel, meals, equipment, supplies, etc.
6. **Home office square footage:** If claiming home office, measure the office space in square feet and note total home square footage.
7. **Business use percentage for shared expenses:** Cell phone, internet, and vehicle -- what portion of actual use is business?
8. **Prior reimbursements:** Has the company ever reimbursed these expenses before? Were prior payments included in your W-2?
9. **State of incorporation and operating state(s):** Some states impose additional rules on reimbursement arrangements.

## Required Documents And Records

### To establish the plan

- **Written accountable plan document:** A signed, dated policy that identifies covered employees, eligible expense categories, required substantiation, and the deadlines (60-day substantiation, 120-day return of excess). While IRS regulations do not explicitly require a written plan, a written document is essential for audit defense and consistent administration.
- **Corporate resolution:** For S-corps and C-corps, a board resolution formally adopting the accountable plan before reimbursements begin. Date it before the first reimbursement.

### To support each reimbursement

- **Expense report:** A completed report for each reimbursement request showing expense category, date, amount, and business purpose. Use the attached "Accountable Plan Reimbursements Implementation Worksheet" for this purpose.
- **Receipts for expenses of $75 or more:** Treasury Regulation 1.274-5(c)(2)(iii) and IRS Publication 463 require a receipt (or other documentary evidence such as a bank statement) for any single expense of $75 or more. Lodging receipts are required regardless of amount.
- **Mileage log for vehicle expenses:** A contemporaneous log recording the date, destination, business purpose, and miles driven for each business trip. Apps or a paper log both qualify.
- **Photograph or floor plan for home office:** A diagram or photo identifying the office space with square footage measurements supports the business-use calculation.
- **Documentation of business purpose:** For meals and travel, note who was present (for meals) or the business destination and purpose (for travel).

### Retention period

Keep expense reports, receipts, and supporting records for a minimum of 3 years from the date you filed the tax return to which they relate (standard statute of limitations). If employment taxes are involved, retain payroll records for at least 4 years after the later of the tax due date or the date paid. Retain records related to depreciable property (such as a home office capital improvement) until the statute expires for the year you dispose of the property.

## Step-By-Step Implementation

### Phase 1: Setup (do once)

**Step 1 -- Confirm eligibility with your advisor.**
Confirm the entity type, your role as an employee, and that the expenses you intend to reimburse qualify as ordinary and necessary business expenses under IRC Section 162. Do not begin until your advisor has reviewed the plan structure.

**Step 2 -- Draft the written accountable plan document.**
Create a written policy that includes:
- The name of the employer entity
- A list of eligible employees (or "all W-2 employees")
- Eligible expense categories (e.g., home office, vehicle mileage, cell phone, internet, travel, meals, supplies)
- The 60-day substantiation deadline
- The 120-day excess-return deadline
- Instructions for completing the expense report form
- Signature lines for the employer and the effective date

Your advisor or attorney can review this document before adoption. A template may be provided in the attached download.

**Step 3 -- Pass a corporate resolution (S-corps and C-corps).**
Hold a meeting of the board of directors (which may be you alone) and pass a resolution stating that the corporation adopts the accountable plan as described in Step 2, effective on a stated date. Sign and date the resolution. File it in the corporate minute book.

**Step 4 -- Identify and calculate recurring expense amounts.**
For each expense category, determine the monthly amount you will submit for reimbursement:

- **Home office:** Divide the office square footage by total home square footage to get the business-use percentage. Apply that percentage to actual monthly expenses: mortgage interest or rent, property taxes, homeowners insurance, utilities, internet (the home-related portion), and depreciation (if applicable). Your advisor must help calculate depreciation. Alternatively, use the simplified IRS method: $5 per square foot, up to a maximum of 300 square feet ($1,500 per year maximum). Note: the simplified method is only available when computing the deduction for a self-employed person on Schedule C; for employer reimbursements the actual expense method using the business-use percentage is the standard approach. Confirm the calculation method with your advisor.
- **Business mileage:** Track miles driven for business. Apply the 2026 IRS standard mileage rate of 72.5 cents per mile (per IRS Notice 2026-10, effective January 1, 2026), or reimburse actual vehicle costs with a detailed log.
- **Cell phone:** Calculate the percentage of actual use that is for business. Apply that percentage to your monthly bill. If your employer requires you to use your personal phone for business for a genuine, noncompensatory business reason (not merely as a bonus), the IRS allows the reimbursement to be treated as nontaxable without strict business-use recordkeeping, per Notice 2011-72.
- **Internet:** Calculate the business-use percentage of your home internet bill and apply it. Note: reimbursement of the basic cost of a primary home telephone or internet line may be taxable; confirm with your advisor.
- **Travel, meals, supplies:** Reimburse actual amounts with receipts.

**Step 5 -- Set a reimbursement cadence.**
Decide whether you will reimburse monthly, biweekly, or with each payroll run. Document the cadence in the written plan. Consistency matters: irregular or lump-sum year-end reimbursements raise audit risk and may fail the "reasonable period of time" requirement.

### Phase 2: Ongoing operation (recurring)

**Step 6 -- Complete an expense report for each period.**
Using the attached "Accountable Plan Reimbursements Implementation Worksheet," record each expense: date, category, amount, and business purpose. Attach receipts for items of $75 or more and for all lodging. For mileage, attach or reference the mileage log.

**Step 7 -- Submit the expense report to the employer within 60 days.**
The employee (you, in the owner-employee scenario) submits the completed expense report and supporting receipts to the employer (your company). Keep a copy in your records.

**Step 8 -- Issue the reimbursement check.**
The company writes a check or initiates an ACH transfer to the employee for the substantiated amount. Record the payment in the company books as a business expense under the applicable category (home office expense, vehicle expense, communications expense, etc.), not as wages.

**Step 9 -- Return any excess within 120 days.**
If the company advanced funds before you incurred the expense, and the advance exceeded the actual substantiated cost, return the difference to the company within 120 days of incurring the expense. Document the return with a receipt or transfer record.

**Step 10 -- Handle W-2 reporting correctly.**
Properly substantiated accountable plan reimbursements are NOT reported as wages in W-2 Box 1. If the company pays a per diem or mileage allowance (a fixed daily rate or per-mile rate rather than actual-cost reimbursements) and the rate is at or below the applicable IRS/GSA federal rate, the amounts are not reported on the W-2. If the per diem or mileage rate EXCEEDS the applicable federal rate, the excess over the federal rate is reported as wages in Box 1, and the nontaxable portion is reported in Box 12 using Code L.

### Phase 3: Annual review

**Step 11 -- Review and update the plan each January.**
Check for updated IRS mileage rates (announced each December), updated GSA per diem rates (effective October 1 each year for the following federal fiscal year), and any changes to your home office arrangement or expense categories. Update the written plan and issue a new corporate resolution if material changes are made.

**Step 12 -- Confirm W-2s and payroll records match the plan.**
Before your payroll service or bookkeeper finalizes W-2s each January, verify that accountable plan reimbursements were excluded from Box 1 wages and that any mileage or per diem allowances exceeding federal rates are correctly included and coded.

## Entity, Payroll, State, And Current-Year Caveats

### Entity-specific caveats

- **S-corp owner-employees:** You must be on payroll receiving a W-2 before accountable plan reimbursements make sense. If the S-corp is not running payroll, accountable plan reimbursements are not available. Confirm payroll is established before proceeding.
- **2% S-corp shareholders:** While you can receive tax-free business expense reimbursements (mileage, home office, phone, travel) under an accountable plan, other fringe benefits (employer-paid health insurance, HSAs, certain other benefits) are subject to different rules and may be taxable. Do not assume that because accountable plan expense reimbursements work, all other fringe benefits work the same way. Confirm with your advisor.
- **C-corporations:** The deduction for the reimbursed expense remains at the corporate level. The employee pays no tax on the reimbursement. The corporate tax benefit may differ from the pass-through benefit in an S-corp.
- **Sole proprietors:** Cannot use an accountable plan for personal reimbursements. Deduct expenses directly on Schedule C.

### Payroll caveats

- **Commingling with wages:** Do not include accountable plan reimbursements in the same check as W-2 wages without clear bookkeeping separation. Keep reimbursement checks and payroll checks separate, or at minimum separate in the books.
- **Payroll tax savings:** Properly excluding accountable plan reimbursements from wages saves both the employer and employee FICA taxes on those amounts. If reimbursements are accidentally run through payroll as wages, the FICA savings are lost and the error may require amended returns.

### State caveats

- **State income tax conformity:** Most states conform to federal accountable plan rules, but not all. Some states may treat certain reimbursements differently. Confirm your state's rules with your advisor, particularly for home office and vehicle expenses.
- **State payroll tax:** Some states impose state unemployment taxes (SUTA) and other payroll taxes differently. Properly excludable accountable plan reimbursements should also be excluded from state wage bases, but confirm with your payroll provider.
- **Multi-state employees:** If you work in multiple states, the home office deduction calculation and allocation may involve multiple state returns.

### Current-year caveats

- **OBBBA permanently eliminates unreimbursed employee expense deductions:** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 4, 2025, Public Law 119-21) permanently eliminated the miscellaneous itemized deduction for unreimbursed employee business expenses for most employees. The small group still permitted to deduct unreimbursed expenses above-the-line (Armed Forces reservists, certain state and local government officials, certain performing artists) is now much narrower. For most W-2 employees, an employer accountable plan is now the only tax-favored route to receive any benefit from business expenses.
- **TCJA provisions made permanent:** The OBBBA made many TCJA provisions permanent, so there is no current sunset risk for the rules underlying this strategy.

## Common Mistakes

1. **No written plan document:** The most common audit failure. If you cannot produce a written plan that predates the first reimbursement, the IRS may treat all reimbursements as taxable wages.

2. **No corporate resolution:** For S-corps and C-corps, failing to pass a board resolution adopting the plan before the first reimbursement is made. Create and date the resolution before any money moves.

3. **Missing receipts:** Reimbursing expenses without receipts for amounts of $75 or more. Lodging has no exception -- always require a receipt.

4. **Late substantiation:** Submitting expense reports months after the expenses were incurred. The safe harbor is 60 days. Consistent late filing can invalidate the plan.

5. **Not returning excess advances:** Receiving a cash advance and then not returning the unspent amount within 120 days. This turns the unreturned amount into taxable wages.

6. **Reimbursing personal expenses:** Including non-business items (personal groceries in a meal receipt, personal trips in the mileage log). Any personal portion must be excluded.

7. **Vague business purpose:** Writing "meeting" or "lunch" on an expense report without identifying who you met with and why. For Section 274(d) expenses (meals, travel), the IRS requires enough detail to establish business purpose.

8. **Running reimbursements through payroll as wages:** If your payroll software treats reimbursements as wages, you lose the FICA exclusion and the employee pays unnecessary income tax. Reimbursements must be processed as expense reimbursements, not wages.

9. **Backdating the corporate resolution:** Creating a resolution after reimbursements have already been made and backdating it. This is improper and, if discovered, can void the plan retroactively.

10. **Ignoring the mileage log:** Claiming vehicle reimbursements without a contemporaneous (kept at the time, not reconstructed later) mileage log. A year-end reconstruction from memory does not satisfy IRS substantiation requirements.

11. **Using the wrong mileage rate:** Applying the prior year's rate rather than the current-year rate. Check the IRS website each January for the updated rate.

## Advisor Review Gates

Do not proceed through the following decisions without first confirming with your advisor:

1. **Before drafting the plan:** Confirm your entity type qualifies, that you are properly on payroll as a W-2 employee, and that the specific expenses you intend to reimburse qualify as ordinary and necessary business expenses under IRC Section 162.

2. **Before adopting the plan:** Have your advisor or attorney review the written accountable plan document and corporate resolution before you sign and date them.

3. **Home office calculation method:** Confirm whether to use the actual-expense method (percentage of home costs) or another approach for employer reimbursement purposes. The simplified $5/sq ft method was designed for Schedule C; its application to employer reimbursements is not directly confirmed in IRS publications.

4. **2% S-corp shareholder fringe benefits:** If you own more than 2% of the S-corp's stock, confirm with your advisor which specific expense categories can be reimbursed tax-free and which cannot, particularly for anything beyond ordinary business expenses (travel, mileage, home office, phone).

5. **State conformity:** Before your first reimbursement, confirm that your state follows federal accountable plan rules and does not impose additional requirements.

6. **W-2 coding review:** Before finalizing W-2s each January, have your advisor or payroll provider confirm that accountable plan reimbursements are excluded from Box 1 and that any per diem or mileage allowances exceeding federal rates are properly coded in Box 12 with Code L.

7. **Material plan changes:** Any time you change the eligible expense categories, the reimbursement method, or the parties covered by the plan, get advisor review before implementing the change.

## Before You Act

**Stop here before you start reimbursing yourself or any employee.**

The benefits of an accountable plan -- tax-free reimbursements, FICA savings, and business deductions -- depend entirely on the plan being set up correctly and documented consistently before any money moves. A plan that looks right but lacks a written document predating the first payment, a corporate resolution, or timely expense reports with adequate receipts can be recharacterized by the IRS as taxable wages, creating back taxes, interest, and penalties.

Before you act:
- Confirm your specific entity structure, payroll setup, and expense categories with your advisor.
- Have your advisor review the written plan document and corporate resolution before you sign them.
- Do not issue the first reimbursement until both documents exist and are dated.
- Confirm your state's treatment with your advisor.
- Confirm the home office calculation method with your advisor before applying it.

   URL: https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/nonresident-aliens-and-the-accountable-plan-rules
   Confirms: Three core requirements; exclusion from gross income when plan rules are met; amounts in excess are reportable on W-2; reference to Revenue Procedure 2019-48.

9. **IRS -- S Corporation Compensation and Medical Insurance Issues**
   URL: https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/s-corporation-compensation-and-medical-insurance-issues
   Confirms: 2% shareholder definition (anyone owning more than 2% of stock or voting power); treatment as partner for fringe benefit purposes.

10. **IRS Notice 2011-72 -- Tax Treatment of Employer-Provided Cell Phones**
    URL: https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-11-72.pdf
    Confirms: Employer cell phone reimbursements are nontaxable when there is a substantial noncompensatory business reason; no strict business-use recordkeeping required in that scenario; still referenced in IRS Publication 15-B (2026).

11. **IRS Publication 15-B (2026) -- Employer's Tax Guide to Fringe Benefits**
    URL: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15b
    Confirms: Working condition fringe benefit rules; 2% S-corp shareholder treatment for fringe benefits; cell phone guidance cross-reference to Notice 2011-72.

12. **IRS Topic 305 -- Recordkeeping**
    URL: https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc305
    Confirms: 3-year retention for general business records; 4-year retention for employment tax records.
