# Ministerial Housing Allowance Review

> Educational guidance for Beyond Freedom Financial clients. Not personalized tax, legal, payroll, or state-law advice. Confirm your specific facts with your advisor before acting.

## Overview

If you are an ordained, commissioned, or licensed minister, IRC Section 107 allows you to exclude a designated portion of your compensation from your federal gross income for income tax purposes -- the "ministerial housing allowance." The excluded amount is never taxed as income at the federal level (and in most states), as long as it does not exceed actual housing costs or the fair rental value of your home. The strategy generates a real dollar tax savings on housing expenses you would pay anyway, but it requires your employing church or organization to formally designate the allowance in writing before you are paid, and it requires you to track and document your actual housing expenses every year. This playbook walks you through how to set up, claim, and maintain the exclusion correctly.

## Who This Applies To

This strategy applies to you if ALL of the following are true:

- You are **ordained, commissioned, or licensed** as a minister by a church, denomination, or qualifying religious organization recognized under federal law.
- You perform duties the IRS considers "ministerial": conducting worship services, administering sacraments (such as baptisms, weddings, funerals), or exercising responsibility in the governance or religious functions of a church or denomination.
- Your **employing church or organization** is willing to officially designate a portion of your compensation as a housing allowance in writing, in advance of payment.
- You have actual, documentable housing costs (rent, mortgage, utilities, repairs, etc.) or live in a home with a determinable fair rental value.
- You receive compensation from ministerial services (salary, honoraria, fees) from which the allowance is designated.

**Bivocational (dual-job) ministers:** You may still qualify, but the housing allowance exclusion applies ONLY to the portion of your income that comes from ministerial duties. Income from a secular employer is not eligible.

**Retired ministers:** You may continue to designate a housing allowance on distributions from a qualifying 403(b)(9) church retirement plan (see the Caveats section for details).

## When This Is Not A Fit

- **You are not ordained, commissioned, or licensed.** Lay employees of a church, administrative staff, and music directors who are not ministerially credentialed do not qualify, even if they do important religious work.
- **Your religious organization has not or will not make a written, advance designation.** The designation must be made BEFORE the compensation is paid. A designation made after the fact has no tax effect, even if it covers the same calendar year. If your organization will not act before payment, the exclusion is not available for that pay period.
- **Your actual housing costs and fair rental value are zero or nominal.** If your church provides free housing (a parsonage) and pays all costs directly, the housing allowance cash exclusion is not the right structure -- the parsonage's fair rental value is still included in your self-employment income but not in your gross income for income tax purposes. This is a different scenario; confirm with your advisor.
- **Your net ministerial earnings are below $400 for the year.** You would have no self-employment tax due and likely no income tax due. The exclusion may have minimal practical benefit at this level, though you can still designate and track.
- **You have already filed for and received an approved Form 4361 exemption from self-employment tax.** In this case you are exempt from self-employment taxes on ministerial income. The housing allowance exclusion still applies to income tax, but the self-employment tax impact described in this playbook does not apply to you. Confirm treatment with your advisor.
- **You are a member of a religious order who has taken a vow of poverty.** Different rules govern your situation. This playbook covers ministers who have not taken a vow of poverty.

## Key Terms

**IRC Section 107:** The section of the Internal Revenue Code (the federal tax statute) that allows a "minister of the gospel" to exclude a housing allowance from gross income for federal income tax purposes. "IRC" stands for Internal Revenue Code.

**Gross income:** All income you receive from any source before any deductions or exclusions. The housing allowance exclusion removes a portion of your ministerial pay from this starting number.

**Housing allowance (also called parsonage allowance or rental allowance):** The portion of a minister's total compensation that is officially designated by the employing church or organization as payment for housing costs. Once properly designated, it is excluded from your federal gross income for income tax purposes (subject to the three-cap test below).

**Designation:** The formal, written, advance action by your employing church or organization that officially labels part of your compensation as a housing allowance. Without a valid designation, no exclusion is available for that income. The designation must: (1) be in writing, (2) specify a dollar amount, and (3) be made BEFORE the pay period in which it applies.

**Three-cap (lesser-of) test:** The IRS limits how much of the designated housing allowance you can actually exclude from income. You may exclude only the SMALLEST of these three amounts:
1. The amount officially designated as your housing allowance by your employer.
2. The actual housing expenses you paid during the year from the allowance.
3. The fair rental value of your home, including furnishings, utilities, and a garage.

If any of these three numbers is lower than the others, that lower number is your maximum exclusion. Any designated amount above the lowest cap is "excess allowance" and must be included in your taxable income.

**Fair rental value (FRV):** What a comparable furnished home in your area would rent for on the open market, including utilities. This is not your mortgage payment or what you actually pay -- it is what the market would charge for a similar home. For a minister who rents, the FRV is typically close to your actual rent. For a homeowner, the FRV requires a judgment call (and sometimes a formal appraisal). Your advisor or a local real estate resource can help estimate FRV.

**Actual housing expenses:** The costs you paid during the year that qualify as housing expenses. See "Required Documents And Records" and "Step-By-Step Implementation" for the full list. Expenses must actually be paid during the tax year -- they cannot be carried forward from a prior year or applied to a future year.

**Self-employment tax (SE tax):** The federal tax that covers Social Security and Medicare for workers who are not subject to FICA withholding (the tax automatically withheld from wages). For tax year 2026, the SE tax rate is 15.3% on net self-employment earnings up to $184,500 (12.4% Social Security plus 2.9% Medicare), and 2.9% on earnings above $184,500. Most ministers pay SE tax rather than FICA because the IRS generally treats ministers as self-employed for Social Security and Medicare purposes, even when employed by a church. The housing allowance exclusion does NOT reduce SE tax -- the housing allowance is included in net self-employment earnings when computing SE tax, even though it is excluded from income tax.

**Schedule SE (Form 1040):** The IRS form you use to calculate your self-employment tax. Ministers report ministerial income (including the housing allowance) on Schedule SE, even though the housing allowance is excluded from gross income on Form 1040.

**Net self-employment earnings:** The amount on which SE tax is calculated. It equals your gross self-employment income (including the housing allowance) minus allowable business deductions, then multiplied by 92.35% (a factor that accounts for the employer-equivalent half of SE tax). The 92.35% multiplier is a statutory adjustment built into Schedule SE; you do not calculate it separately.

**FICA:** Federal Insurance Contributions Act taxes -- the Social Security and Medicare taxes paid by employees and employers. Most ministers are NOT subject to FICA on ministerial income; they pay SE tax instead.

**Form 4361:** The IRS form a minister can file to apply for an exemption from self-employment tax on ministerial earnings. The exemption is granted only if you have genuine religious or conscientious objections to accepting public insurance benefits (Social Security, Medicare) and is irrevocable once granted. If you hold an approved Form 4361 exemption, you do not pay SE tax on ministerial income, but you also do not build Social Security or Medicare credits from that income.

**Form W-2:** IRS Form W-2, Wage and Tax Statement. If you are an employee of a church, your W-2 Box 1 (Wages) should NOT include the designated housing allowance. The church may voluntarily report the housing allowance amount in Box 14 (Other) as an informational item. Box 3 (Social Security Wages) and Box 5 (Medicare Wages) should be blank for a minister's compensation because ministers are generally not subject to FICA.

**Form 1040 / Form 1040-SR:** The federal individual income tax return. Any excess housing allowance (the portion above your exclusion cap) is reported as additional wages on Line 1h of Form 1040 or 1040-SR, with the notation "Excess allowance" written on the dotted line.

**Form 1040-ES:** The IRS form for calculating and paying quarterly estimated taxes. Because churches typically do not withhold income tax or SE tax from ministers' pay, ministers must make estimated tax payments to avoid underpayment penalties.

**Schedule A (Form 1040):** The itemized deductions form. Ministers who own their homes may deduct mortgage interest and real estate taxes on Schedule A even if those same expenses were paid with housing allowance money. This is an explicit statutory exception under IRC Section 265(a)(6) -- the normal rule that prohibits deducting expenses paid with tax-exempt funds does not apply to ministers' housing allowances. This potential "double benefit" is available only if you itemize deductions rather than taking the standard deduction.

**IRC Section 265(a)(6):** The specific statutory exception that allows ministers to deduct mortgage interest and real estate taxes on Schedule A even when those costs were paid from an excluded housing allowance.

**403(b)(9) church plan:** A type of retirement account available to ministers employed by churches. The "(9)" designation means the plan is set up specifically for church employees and preserves the minister's right to designate retirement distributions as a housing allowance in retirement. Withdrawals from a 403(b)(9) plan used for housing can continue to qualify for the IRC Section 107 exclusion, if the plan administrator designates them as a housing allowance before the year begins.

**Reasonable compensation:** The IRS requires that the housing allowance not exceed reasonable pay for the ministerial services you performed. If your total compensation (salary plus housing allowance) is out of proportion to your ministerial duties, the IRS may challenge the exclusion on the grounds that the housing portion exceeds reasonable pay.

**Parsonage:** A home owned by the church and provided directly to the minister to live in. If you live in a church-owned parsonage, the fair rental value of the parsonage is included in SE tax calculations but excluded from gross income for income tax purposes. Cash housing allowances and in-kind parsonage provision are related but distinct arrangements. This playbook focuses on the cash housing allowance.

## Required Client Inputs

Gather these facts before beginning or reviewing your housing allowance:

1. **Ministerial credential status:** Confirm you are ordained, commissioned, or licensed and have documentation of that credential (ordination certificate, commissioning letter, or denominational license).
2. **Employing organization's information:** Name of the church or organization, the name and contact of whoever manages board resolutions or designations, and confirmation that the organization is recognized as a church or qualifying religious body.
3. **Prior year W-2 and tax return:** To confirm how the housing allowance was reported (or whether it was reported at all) and to identify any prior issues.
4. **Projected total annual housing expenses:** Estimated amounts for the coming year, broken down by category (see eligible expense list under Step-By-Step Implementation). This is what you give your employer to determine how much to designate.
5. **Fair rental value estimate for your home:** An estimate of what your furnished home including utilities would rent for per month on the open market. For homeowners, a real estate agent or a rental listing comparison in your area can provide a starting estimate.
6. **Total ministerial compensation:** Your salary, stipends, honoraria, and any other ministerial pay (before the housing designation). The housing allowance cannot exceed your total compensation or the IRS's reasonable compensation standard.
7. **SE tax exemption status:** Whether you have a previously approved Form 4361 exemption. If you are unsure, your advisor can look up your IRS records.
8. **State of residence:** State tax treatment of the housing allowance varies. Your advisor needs your state to confirm whether the exclusion applies at the state level.
9. **Retirement plan type:** If you are retired or near retirement, confirm whether your church retirement plan is a 403(b)(9) church plan, which is the only type that preserves the housing allowance designation in retirement.

## Required Documents And Records

**Create and keep permanently:**
- Written board resolution or other official document from the employing church or organization designating the housing allowance amount for the current year, signed and dated BEFORE the first pay period it covers.
- Your ordination, commissioning, or licensing credential document.
- Any employment contract that includes a housing allowance provision.

**Track and retain for at least 3 years after filing the return to which they relate (6 years if income understated by 25% or more; 7 years for loss-related items):**
- Monthly or annual log of actual housing expenses paid, with the category, date, and amount for each item.
- Receipts, statements, or invoices supporting each housing expense (mortgage statement, utility bills, insurance statements, repair invoices).
- Documentation of your home's fair rental value for the year (rental listing comparisons, agent letter, or other market evidence).
- A year-end worksheet reconciling: (1) the designated amount, (2) actual expenses paid, and (3) fair rental value, to determine the actual excludable amount and any excess that must be included in income.
- Copies of your W-2 (confirming the housing allowance was NOT included in Box 1).
- Copies of your Form 1040 showing how the exclusion was reported (and any excess allowance reported on Line 1h).

**If you are a homeowner, retain until 3 years after the year you sell the home:**
- Mortgage statements (Form 1098 from your lender) showing annual interest paid.
- Real estate tax bills and payment receipts.
- Records of home improvements that affect your basis in the home.

**Record retention general rule:** The IRS can generally assess additional tax for up to 3 years from the filing date. That window extends to 6 years if you underreport gross income by more than 25%, and there is no limit if the return is fraudulent. Keep housing allowance records for at least 6 years as a conservative baseline, given the potential for IRS questions about the exclusion amount.

## Step-By-Step Implementation

### Phase 1: Confirm Eligibility (Do First)

**Step 1: Verify your ministerial credential.**
Locate your ordination certificate, commissioning letter, or denominational license. Confirm your current credential is active and issued by a recognized religious body. If your credential has lapsed or if your duties have changed (for example, you moved to an administrative role with no ministerial functions), the exclusion may not apply. Confirm your status with your advisor.

**Step 2: Identify your qualifying housing expenses for the coming year.**
List every housing cost you expect to pay. The following are eligible expenses under 26 CFR 1.107-1:
- Rent payments (if you rent)
- Mortgage principal and interest payments (if you own)
- Down payment on a home purchase (allowed under the regulation's general "amounts used to provide a home" standard; not enumerated as a named expense in 26 CFR 1.107-1 -- confirm treatment with your advisor)
- Property taxes and homeowner's insurance premiums
- Utilities: electricity, gas, water, sewer, trash, internet used for housing
- Home repairs and maintenance (painting, plumbing, roof repair, HVAC)
- Furniture, appliances, and household furnishings
- Home improvements (kitchen remodel, new flooring)
- Home equity loan payments, to the extent used for the home
- Lawn care and pest control
- HOA (homeowners association) fees

The following are NOT eligible:
- Groceries and food
- Domestic household employees (housekeepers, nannies)
- Costs of a second or vacation home
- Costs unrelated to your primary housing

**Step 3: Estimate the fair rental value of your home.**
Research comparable furnished rentals in your area, including utilities, to estimate the monthly FRV of your home. For a renter, your actual rent is usually close to the FRV. For a homeowner, this requires a market comparison -- check local rental listings for homes of similar size, features, and location. Write down your estimated monthly FRV and annualize it (monthly FRV x 12).

**Step 4: Calculate your projected annual designation amount.**
Add up your projected eligible housing expenses for the year. Compare that to your annualized FRV estimate. Your designation should be set at or slightly above the larger of these two figures, but must not exceed your total annual ministerial compensation. Setting the designation too low means you leave money on the table. Setting it above your total compensation or FRV means part of it becomes taxable as excess allowance.

### Phase 2: Obtain the Formal Designation (Before First Pay Period)

**Step 5: Request the written designation from your church or organization.**
Provide your church board, finance committee, or denominational administrator with your projected annual housing expense list (Step 2) and your recommended designation amount (Step 4). The organization must formally adopt the designation before your first paycheck for the period it covers.

**Step 6: Confirm the written designation meets all requirements.**
The designation document must:
- Be in writing.
- Identify you by name (or by ministerial role) as the recipient.
- State a specific dollar amount (not a percentage and not "whatever the IRS allows").
- Be dated and signed (or formally adopted by board action) BEFORE the pay period it applies to.

Valid forms of designation include: a board resolution recorded in official minutes, a provision in your employment contract, an item in the approved church budget, or any other official organizational action in writing made in advance. An informal email or verbal agreement is NOT sufficient.

**Step 7: File and retain the designation document.**
Keep the original signed resolution or contract provision permanently. Make a copy for your personal tax records. This document is your first line of defense if the IRS questions the exclusion.

**Step 8: Confirm W-2 setup with your church's payroll administrator.**
The housing allowance amount should NOT appear in Box 1 (Wages) of your W-2. The church may optionally report it in Box 14 (Other) labeled "Housing Allowance" as an informational item. Boxes 3 and 5 (Social Security and Medicare Wages) should be blank because ministers are generally not subject to FICA. If your W-2 includes the housing allowance in Box 1, contact your church's payroll administrator to correct it before filing.

### Phase 3: Track Actual Expenses Throughout the Year

**Step 9: Set up a housing expense log.**
Create a simple log (spreadsheet, notebook, or accounting software) to record every eligible housing expense as you pay it. For each entry, note: date paid, vendor/payee, category (mortgage, utilities, insurance, repairs, etc.), and dollar amount. Attach or file the receipt or statement for each item.

**Step 10: At year-end, total your actual housing expenses.**
Add up all eligible housing expenses from your log. This is the "actual expenses" figure in the three-cap test.

### Phase 4: Calculate the Excludable Amount at Year-End

**Step 11: Apply the three-cap (lesser-of) test.**
Write down all three figures:
- Cap 1: The designated amount (from Step 5).
- Cap 2: Your actual housing expenses paid during the year (from Step 10).
- Cap 3: The fair rental value of your home for the year (Step 3, annualized).

Your excludable housing allowance is the SMALLEST of these three numbers.

**Step 12: Determine the excess allowance, if any.**
If the designated amount (Cap 1) is greater than the smaller of Cap 2 and Cap 3, the difference is "excess allowance." Excess allowance must be included in your income for the year. Report the excess on Form 1040, Line 1h, with the notation "Excess allowance" on the dotted line.

**Step 13: Calculate your SE tax base.**
For SE tax purposes, add the full housing allowance amount (the designated amount, up to your actual exclusion plus any excess) back into your net self-employment earnings on Schedule SE. The housing allowance exclusion provides no relief from SE tax.

### Phase 5: Report on Your Tax Return

**Step 14: Report income, the exclusion, and SE tax.**
Work with your tax preparer to:
- Confirm the total ministerial compensation (wages or self-employment income) is reported correctly.
- Confirm the housing allowance exclusion is applied (the excluded amount should NOT be in your gross income on Form 1040).
- Report any excess allowance on Line 1h.
- Include the housing allowance in the Schedule SE calculation.
- Deduct one-half of self-employment tax as an adjustment to income on Schedule 1, Line 15 of Form 1040 (this is the "employer-equivalent deduction" that partially offsets SE tax).

**Step 15: Consider the Schedule A double benefit (homeowners who itemize).**
If you own your home and you itemize deductions on Schedule A, you may deduct mortgage interest and real estate taxes even if you paid those costs from your housing allowance. This is permitted under IRC Section 265(a)(6). This benefit only applies if your total itemized deductions exceed the standard deduction. Confirm with your advisor before claiming both the exclusion and these deductions.

**Step 16: Make quarterly estimated tax payments.**
Because your church typically does not withhold income tax or SE tax from your pay, you must pay taxes in advance using estimated payments. Use Form 1040-ES. The four due dates for 2026 estimated payments are: April 15, 2026; June 15, 2026; September 15, 2026; and January 15, 2027. Underpaying can result in an underpayment penalty.

### Phase 6: Annual Reset

**Step 17: Repeat the designation process before each new tax year.**
A housing allowance designation applies only for the period it covers. Before January 1 of each year, request a new written designation for the coming year. If your housing costs are likely to increase, adjust the designated amount upward. Do not assume last year's designation automatically rolls over.

**Step 18: If your circumstances change mid-year, request an amended designation.**
If you move, refinance, buy a home, or your housing costs change significantly, your church can amend the designation prospectively (from the date of the amendment forward). Amendments do not apply retroactively to prior months. The amendment must itself be in writing and in advance of future payments.

## Entity, Payroll, State, And Current-Year Caveats

**Your organization must be a qualifying religious employer.** The housing allowance exclusion is available only when a church, denomination, or qualified religious organization designates the allowance. If you perform ministerial services for a for-profit company or a non-religious nonprofit, the exclusion does not apply to that income.

**Bivocational ministers: the exclusion applies only to ministerial income.** If you also hold secular employment, the housing allowance can be designated only from your ministerial compensation. You cannot allocate housing allowance against wages from a non-ministerial employer.

**SE tax is unavoidable unless you hold Form 4361.** The housing allowance exclusion does not reduce your SE tax base. Ministers frequently underestimate this liability because it is not withheld from pay. The 15.3% SE tax on 92.35% of net ministerial earnings (including the housing allowance) is substantial. Set aside funds throughout the year and make quarterly estimated payments.

**The Additional Medicare Tax may apply.** If your combined wages and net self-employment income exceed $200,000 (single filers) or $250,000 (married filing jointly), an additional 0.9% Medicare tax applies to the excess. This is computed on Form 8959 and reported on your Form 1040.

**Retroactive designations are void.** If your church did not designate the housing allowance before a pay period, you cannot claim the exclusion for that period, even if you amend the designation later. There is no cure for a missing advance designation. Going forward, ensure the designation is in place before each pay period.

**Mid-year amendments apply only from the date of amendment.** If you request an increased designation mid-year because your housing costs rose (you bought a home, moved, or started a major repair project), the higher designation applies only to pay periods after the amendment is adopted. Amounts paid before the amendment are governed by the original designation.

**State income tax treatment varies and is not uniform.** The federal exclusion under IRC Section 107 is a federal rule only. States set their own rules. Most states that use federal adjusted gross income (AGI) as their starting point automatically follow the federal exclusion, because the housing allowance is not in federal AGI. However, some states -- including Pennsylvania -- impose state income tax on the housing allowance regardless of the federal exclusion. Confirm your state's treatment with your advisor before assuming the federal exclusion also eliminates your state income tax on the allowance.

**Denominational vs. local designation for employed ministers.** If you are employed by a local congregation, only the local congregation can designate your housing allowance. A blanket resolution from a national denominational body is not effective for ministers employed at the local level. If you are employed directly by a national denomination or denominational agency, the national body's resolution is effective.

**403(b)(9) plans and retirement housing allowance.** Only a 403(b)(9) church plan preserves the retired minister's ability to designate retirement distributions as a housing allowance. If you roll your retirement savings from a 403(b)(9) into a traditional IRA or 401(k), you permanently lose the ability to designate those funds as a housing allowance in retirement. Do not roll out of a 403(b)(9) without first understanding this consequence. The 403(b)(9) plan administrator must make the housing allowance designation before the year in which distributions are received.

**The exclusion does not reduce SE tax in retirement.** Even in retirement, housing allowance distributions from a 403(b)(9) are excluded from income tax if properly designated, but the retired minister who is still performing ministerial services may still owe SE tax on those distributions. Confirm your retirement SE tax obligations with your advisor.

**Form 4361 exemption is irrevocable.** If you file for and receive a SE tax exemption under Form 4361, you permanently give up Social Security and Medicare credits for ministerial income. This affects retirement benefits, disability benefits, and survivor benefits. This decision should not be made without fully understanding the long-term financial impact on your retirement income.

**One Big Beautiful Bill Act (P.L. 119-21, signed July 4, 2025).** This legislation permanently extended the suspension of miscellaneous itemized deductions for tax years beginning after December 31, 2025. Unreimbursed employee business expenses are permanently nondeductible. This does not directly affect the housing allowance exclusion, but it does mean ministers cannot offset compensation income with miscellaneous deductions on their personal return.

## Common Mistakes

**1. No written designation before the year (or pay period) begins.** This is the single most common error. If your church never formally adopted a housing allowance designation before paying you, the exclusion is unavailable for that income, period. The fix going forward is a proper designation; the income already paid without a designation cannot be retroactively excluded.

**2. Missing receipts and no expense log.** Ministers often rely on memory or vague estimates to support the "actual expenses" cap. If audited, you must produce documentation for every dollar of housing expense you relied on to support the exclusion. Lack of receipts can cause the IRS to reduce your exclusion to zero or to fair rental value only.

**3. Designating an amount that exceeds fair rental value.** Setting the designation at an amount larger than what the home would rent for on the open market means part of the designated amount is automatically excess allowance (taxable). The FRV cap is real and enforced. Do not designate an amount so large that it materially exceeds FRV without confirming the FRV estimate is accurate.

**4. Forgetting to include the housing allowance in SE tax.** Many ministers (and some tax preparers) overlook the SE tax inclusion. The result is an understatement of SE tax, interest, and potential penalties. The housing allowance exclusion applies only to income tax, not to SE tax.

**5. Failing to pay quarterly estimated taxes.** Without payroll withholding, ministers who do not make quarterly estimated payments often face a large tax bill plus an underpayment penalty at filing. Use Form 1040-ES and make payments by each quarterly due date.

**6. Treating the housing allowance as SE tax-free after receiving Form 4361.** Some ministers who have Form 4361 exemptions forget it is irrevocable and apply it only to the current year, or misunderstand its scope. Form 4361 eliminates SE tax on ministerial earnings; it does not affect the income tax exclusion. The two rules operate independently.

**7. Including ineligible expenses (food, domestic help) in the actual expense total.** Groceries, housekeeper pay, and similar costs are not eligible housing expenses. Including them inflates your "actual expenses" figure and can trigger IRS challenges to the entire exclusion.

**8. Rolling a 403(b)(9) into an IRA or 401(k) without realizing the consequences.** Moving church retirement funds to a non-church plan permanently eliminates the ability to designate those distributions as a housing allowance in retirement. This mistake often cannot be undone.

**9. Claiming the Schedule A double benefit without itemizing.** The mortgage interest and property tax deduction (under IRC Section 265(a)(6)) only helps you if your total itemized deductions exceed the standard deduction. If you take the standard deduction, the double benefit is irrelevant for that year.

**10. Not updating the designation after a major housing change.** If you move, buy a home, or experience a major cost change mid-year, your prior designation amount may be significantly too low. Request an amended designation prospectively as soon as possible.

## Advisor Review Gates

Pause and confirm with your advisor before acting at any of the following points:

1. **Before claiming the exclusion for the first time:** Confirm that your ministerial credential, your employer's qualifying status, and the written designation are all in place and sufficient before you exclude any amount from income.

2. **Before setting the designated amount each year:** Your advisor should review your projected housing expenses and FRV estimate to help set a designation that maximizes the exclusion without creating excess allowance.

3. **Before considering a Form 4361 exemption:** The irrevocable nature of this election and its lifetime impact on Social Security and Medicare benefits require careful financial planning review before filing.

4. **Before rolling over a 403(b)(9) retirement plan:** Confirm with your advisor whether the plan is a 403(b)(9) church plan and what the consequence of a rollout would be for your future housing allowance in retirement.

5. **Before claiming the Schedule A double benefit:** Your advisor should confirm you are itemizing, that the expenses claimed on Schedule A were actually paid from housing allowance funds, and that you qualify under IRC Section 265(a)(6).

6. **If you change states:** State conformity to the federal housing allowance exclusion varies. Get state-specific confirmation before assuming the exclusion applies.

7. **If you receive a W-2 that incorrectly includes the housing allowance in Box 1:** Request a corrected W-2 (Form W-2c) from your employer. Do not file with an incorrect W-2 without guidance from your advisor.

8. **If the IRS sends a notice questioning your housing allowance exclusion:** Do not respond without your advisor's involvement. Housing allowance audits often hinge on documentation that must be assembled carefully.

9. **If you are retired or approaching retirement:** Confirm the type of retirement plan you hold, the designation procedure for retirement distributions, and your SE tax obligations in retirement.

## Before You Act

This page teaches the ministerial housing allowance strategy and how to implement it. Before you request a designation, exclude any amount from your income, adjust your W-2, make changes to your estimated tax payments, or change your retirement plan, confirm your specific facts with your advisor. The designation must be in place before any pay period it covers. The three-cap test requires documented facts unique to your housing situation. State tax treatment may differ from federal. The Form 4361 exemption, once granted, is irrevocable. Do not act on the strategy until the written designation is confirmed, your actual expense documentation system is in place, and your advisor has reviewed your specific situation.
